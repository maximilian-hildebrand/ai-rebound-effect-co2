\title[Efficiency as a Trap]{Efficiency as a Trap -- To What
Extent Does the Rebound Effect Amplify the
CO\protect\textsubscript{2} Footprint of AI Systems?}
\author[1]{Maximilian Hildebrand}{maximilian.hildebrand@oth-regensburg.de}{}
\affil[1]{OTH Regensburg\\Galgenbergstra\ss{}e 30\\93053 Regensburg\\Germany}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Efficiency gains through AI-driven processes in logistics, energy supply, and production are considered a contribution to reducing CO\textsubscript{2} emissions. This paper examines whether this expectation is empirically sustainable by analysing direct and indirect rebound effects through the theoretical lens of the Jevons Paradox. It is argued that declining costs resulting from efficiency gains expand the use of AI systems -- both directly through increasing query volumes and indirectly through growing production and consumption in other sectors. These rebound effects can systematically compensate or exceed the ecological benefits of AI deployment. Technological progress alone does not constitute a sufficient condition for an improved CO\textsubscript{2} balance.
\end{abstract}
